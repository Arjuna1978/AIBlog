This 2024 landmark case (Moffatt v. Air Canada) in British Columbia’s Civil Resolution should be a powerful message to every business leader: companies are directly responsible for any misinformation their AI systems create.(1)

The dispute was with Air Canada. Their chatbot told a customer they had a 90-day window to apply for a bereavement travel refund, which was completely wrong and directly contradicted the airline's official policy. When the customer was denied the refund, they took the airline to court and won.

Air Canada's defence was remarkable! They argued the chatbot was a "separate legal entity" that they shouldn't be held accountable for. The tribunal completely rejected this idea. They ruled the airline is "responsible for all the information on its website," whether it's from a human or a chatbot, and couldn't understand why a customer would have to "double-check information found in one part of its website on another part."

This decision has huge consequences for anyone using AI. It establishes that an AI system isn't some separate being; it's a direct extension of your company. You have the same legal responsibilities for what your AI says as you would for a human employee. The case was a clear example of "negligent misrepresentation," a legal concept now firmly applied to AI chatbots.

And this problem is only made worse by a core limitation of AI: its tendency to "hallucinate." A recent study from Stanford researchers revealed that even highly specialised legal AIs, which use RAG to improve accuracy, still hallucinate an alarming amount of the time. Some of these models produced incorrect information more than 17% of the time, with one model making things up over 34% of the time (2). RAG systems continue to be sensitive to:

- Source data inaccuracies
- Retrieval insufficiencies
- Bias amplification
- Contextual misalignment

This is a cautionary tale l for leaders, while AI technology is enticing, it must be approached systematically. The conversation has shifted from the theoretical risks of AI to very real, very concrete corporate accountability and financial liability. Simply launching a chatbot isn't enough anymore. You need a rigorous governance and auditing process to ensure your AI is accurate, verifiable, and legally compliant.

Ignoring these risks could turn a small claims court loss into a much bigger financial and reputational disaster.
